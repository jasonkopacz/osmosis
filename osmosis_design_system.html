Osmosis · Design System · v3.0

Learn a language by soaking in it.

Osmosis quietly replaces words on the pages you already read with translations, and gets out of the way. This is the visual language behind that — the colors, type, tokens and components that make the soak feel natural.

Updated May 2026 · Theme Deep ocean (default) · Surface Chrome MV3 popup & in-page DOM · Languages 50+

06Flagship

The Osmotic Swap.

The single defining behavior of the product: a real word, in real prose, replaced inline. Hover reveals the original. Color marks how new the swap is. The treatment must read as a deliberate part of the page — not a Chrome extension overlay.

From "How language soaks in"

You don't apprendre a language by sitting down to study it. You learn it the way you learned your first one: by repeatedly bumping into the same mots in different contextes, each time with just enough scaffolding to guess.

Osmosis takes that idea and runs it on every page you already lis. We pick a small percentage of éligibles words, swap them for translations, and step à part. Hover anything to recover the original; the rest of the page is untouched.

Over weeks, the same mot shows up in dozens of phrases. Your brain does what it always does — it builds meaning from répétition.

density 65%
fresh learning known
1 · Tokenize

Walker traverses the DOM, skipping form fields, code, and editable nodes. Eligible words are scored by frequency rank.

2 · Sample

A configurable percentage (the slider) is sampled — biased toward words the user hasn't seen yet, weighted by rarity.

3 · Swap

Each chosen node is replaced with a .swap span carrying both translation and original. Color encodes recency.

Try it.
Hover any colored word to reveal the original — that's the entire interaction surface.

07Voice

Quiet, confident, never gamified.

Osmosis is the opposite of streak-fire flashcard apps. We don't shout, we don't shame, we don't guilt. The product disappears into the user's day; the copy does the same. Speak like the ocean, not the alarm clock.

Dotone · steady
"You read 8,400 words this week. We translated 412 of them."
Plain numbers, past tense, no exclamation. The user is the subject of the verb.
Don'ttone · gamified
"🔥 8-day streak! Don't lose it — translate 100 more words today!"
Streaks, fire emoji, urgency. We are not Duolingo. The whole point is no pressure.
Docopy · feature
"Replacement frequency — how often we swap words on the pages you read."
Names the setting and what it does in one sentence. No "easy", "simple", or "powerful".
Don'tcopy · feature
"Unlock smart, AI-powered word substitutions — effortlessly!"
Adjective stack, hidden mechanic, marketing voice. The user can't tell what changes when they move the slider.
Doerror · limit
"You've hit the free monthly cap (50,000 chars). Replacements pause until June 1, or you can upgrade."
States the fact, names the date, gives the option without pushing it.
Don'terror · limit
"Oh no! You've run out of translations 😢 Upgrade to keep learning!"
Emoji, performative sympathy, vague "keep learning" instead of "replacements pause."
